# Public Qwen RMS repair feasibility

This is a prospective design, not a replacement candidate or an encrypted implementation. It reads only the archived 113 public-weight native domains, the frozen candidate2 circuit/manifest and the public learned-normalization bound report. It does not read calibration/validation token records, hidden activations or model outputs. The previous calibration passes and independent-validation failures remain unchanged.

## A uniform exact-real circuit exists, but its numerical transfer is unresolved

For a declared positive interval `[a,b]`, choose the public dyadic seed `y0=2^-k`, where `4^k>=b` minimally. Define `z=y/1024` and `w=(1-x*y²)/4`. All 113 native domains have the same positive epsilon and satisfy `1024²*a>=1`. Hence the exact states satisfy `0<=z<=1`, `0<=w<1/4`, and

```
z_next = z*(1+2*w)
w_next = w*w*(3+4*w).
```

The identity `1-x*(1024*z)²=4*w` is preserved in exact real arithmetic. The residual obeys `r_next=r²*(3+r)/4`. The plan encloses this recurrence with directed decimal arithmetic from the exact rational initial residual, and stops at a conservative relative inverse-sqrt error `R/(2-R)<=1e-6`. This is a uniform interval certificate for the symbolic dyadic seed and exact operations. The artifact deliberately does not publish a replacement candidate coefficient manifest.

The exact-native lower epsilon permits an inverse near 1000. Materializing it is unnecessary: multiply the original feature by `z`, then apply public weight `1024*gamma`. The intermediate normalized feature has L2 bound `sqrt(D)/1024`. The learned output remains large where gamma is large; the existing exact-real Qwen learned-norm bound reaches 6144. Scaling the inverse therefore does not establish a small bound for all later ciphertexts.

This tracked form has a serious precision obstacle. Near the lower endpoint of wide intervals, the difference `1/4-w0=a*y0²/4` is extremely small. The script records a plain binary64 lower-endpoint experiment alongside the exact certificate. When initialization rounds to `w0=1/4`, the residual update is exactly stuck while `z` keeps growing by 1.5. Those results are retained as failures of a naive numerical transfer, not removed from the uniform ideal proof. A uniform bound on additive error, and preservation of the correlation between the states, are necessary before using this circuit in any new approximate model or HE implementation. These binary64 failures concern the prospective tracked circuit; they are not additional measurements of the frozen candidate2 model.

On the exact invariant interval, `f(w)=w²(3+4w)` has `max|f'|=9/4`. A local perturbation can therefore grow in the early iterations. For perturbed `z,w`, an elementary step bound includes `1.5*E_z+2*Z*E_w+2*E_z*E_w+rho_z`; a larger interval needs a corresponding larger Lipschitz bound. Refreshing either state introduces another perturbation and does not preserve the identity automatically. The present full-slot refresh component gate of `1e-3` is not a certificate for the tiny residual gap or a `1e-6` normalization result.

## Concrete level schedule, with actual alignment work counted

The prospective state macro uses the existing multiply and public weighted-sum semantics. Initialization of `w0` is one public affine. For each update, compute `w²` and `3+4w` in parallel, multiply, then perform a genuine public unit alignment. Compute `1+2w`, multiply by the old `z`, then genuinely align. The first `z1` is a public affine of `w0`. No scale metadata is relabeled.

This deliberately conservative macro uses three critical levels per update, `3*n+1` through the retained final states, and `3*n+5` for variance formation plus the final feature product/learned affine. It performs `3*n-1` ciphertext products and `4*n` public weighted operations in the inverse. A possible two-level tracked macro is only a symbolic alternative: repeated product scales require a proved scale plan, so its smaller depth is not used to claim executable capacity.

The report enumerates every `w/z` branch at refreshed capacities 25, 28, 29 and 31. It refreshes both available states before another three-level update would leave fewer than four levels. Every refresh input remains at least level 2. The final feature product and learned affine leave at least level 2. Counts are exact for this stated scheduler, conditional on the refresh returning the stated capacity and acceptable error. They are not measured timings, memory peaks, or a claim that the existing refresh implementation preserves the residual relation. Batched packing of independent sites and specialized fused/aligned implementations could change counts and is not assumed.

## What transfers the native public bounds to an approximate graph

The archived 113 envelopes describe the exact native algebra, not the replacement trajectory. A new inductive envelope must establish all of the following without fitting observed validation values:

1. Each variance lies in the new declared interval and its norm polynomial/circuit has a uniform relative multiplier bound `|sqrt(v)*p(v)-1|<=delta`. This gives pre-gamma L2 at most `(1+delta)*sqrt(D)`. Q/K variance bounds then acquire the square of the relevant factor; V, gate and up bounds acquire the relevant single factors. Independently perturbed variance is a separate premise.
2. Attention weights are nonnegative with a certified row-L1 bound. For the frozen exact-real softmax circuit, the actual degree8 power polynomial is uniformly positive on `[-.5,.5]` by coefficient difference from Taylor plus a Taylor remainder. The frozen rounded first-affine reciprocal certificates are rechecked for every K1..32. The initial public `float(1/K)` introduces a small exact rational factor; it is explicitly included. Positive initial mass still places every first squared denominator in `[.5/K,2]`. Later normalization with the **same squared values** in numerator and denominator has mass `1-r` in `[1-1e-12,1]`. Thus the final exact-real row-L1 is at most 1 after at least one frozen squaring. This proof is conditional on the score/exp domains, exact correlation, and no later operation or refresh error.
3. Frozen centered-score intervals are not automatically global public intervals. A coarse input-independent Q/K gamma bound with exact unitary RoPE gives `|score|<=sqrt(128)*max|gamma_q|*max|gamma_k|*(1+delta)^2`; centering K32 costs at most `2*(31/32)`. The report compares this public enclosure with the frozen intervals and exp scaling. Failure of containment is a certificate gap, not an observed model violation. Rounded RoPE and CKKS arithmetic need additional terms.
4. SiLU needs an output envelope as well as an input interval. For the exact native function, `|SiLU(g)|<=|g|`. A rational-tanh approximation can retain this envelope if its base polynomial is uniformly in `[-1,1]` and each positive reciprocal/doubling preserves that interval. The balanced polynomial sites need their own uniform output/error certificate. Candidate2 sampled grids alone do not supply it. Its public input domains with 9/8 margin can accommodate a new norm factor `1+1e-6`, but that fact alone does not close the rest of the graph.
5. Residual envelopes must be recomputed using these factors, the attention row-L1 bound and the approximate SiLU bounds. It is invalid to insert the exact-native upper endpoint into every approximate norm without this induction. Floating/CKKS square/reduction error also requires an explicit positive epsilon margin; exact epsilon positivity is not a finite-precision certificate.

## Tighter public inequalities and the next useful evidence

The first normalization can use exact minimum/maximum row norms over the entire public embedding table, rather than the coordinatewise maximum vector followed by a sum. This is input independent and could give a useful positive lower endpoint for that one site. Later sites can replace Frobenius/absolute-row composition by rigorously enclosed spectral or Gram bounds. Reverse-triangle residual lower bounds are only useful when the retained residual's lower norm exceeds the entire update upper norm; otherwise epsilon is the only certified floor. For Q/K head projections, the rectangular map has a null space in the unrestricted input envelope, so a positive variance lower bound above epsilon does not follow merely from an input norm bound. Reachability information would be needed. None of these observations establishes practical later-layer intervals yet.

Recomputing `x*y²` in an untracked Newton update avoids permanently retaining the rounded fixed-point residual; its usual static schedule costs four levels per update after the first affine. It still needs either a very large retained variance or a public rescaling with correspondingly tiny correlated products. For instance, reducing x by its public upper endpoint moves the low endpoint toward `a/b`; a refresh absolute-error bound must resolve that scale. A bounded state magnitude alone therefore does not establish sufficient HE precision.

The smallest sound next step is public evidence, not another full-model run: compute the exact embedding-row interval and complete the approximate-graph induction obligations (especially global score scaling and SiLU output envelopes), then test a fixed endpoint/interior scalar implementation with a proved perturbation budget. The wide-interval tracked scheme's recorded binary64 cancellation shows why direct promotion would be premature. If cancellation cannot be controlled, analyze a different scaled Newton formulation or a public multiscale polynomial scheme and its full cost before selecting a new candidate. No optimality or practicality claim is made here; the independently frozen untouched input set must remain unused until a future candidate and all prerequisites are fixed.
